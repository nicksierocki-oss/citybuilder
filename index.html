How to play

  1. Extend the regional road (the avenue entering from the west edge). Only roads connected to a map edge count.
  2. Zone beside roads: Residential, Commercial, Industrial, plus Offices (skilled workers), Farms and Mixed-use. Drag to paint rectangles.
  3. Watch demand (bottom-left): homes need jobs, shops need residents, industry needs workers & customers.
  4. Power & water: build a wind farm or coal plant and a water pump (best near the river) beside a road. They flow along roads. Buildings need power for medium density and water for high.
  5. Keep people happy with schools, clinics, plazas, parks and clean air. Recycling centers and trees cut pollution.
  6. Stay safe: police stations cut crime (dense, run-down and jobless areas attract it). Fire stations prevent fires and put them out; without one nearby a fire can spread and burn buildings down. Click a fire alert to jump to it.
  7. Keep traffic moving: drag the Upgrade tool (9) along busy roads: Street → Avenue → Highway (no driveways). Intersections slow cars down: add a Roundabout or Lights at busy ones, and Ramps (an interchange) where a highway crosses another road. One-way streets carry more: drag in the direction of flow, and pair them up so cars can get back (the Traffic overlay shows cut-off roads in purple).
  8. Public transit: in the Lines panel make a bus or tram line, then click stops in order (clicking land beside a road builds a stop). People within 3 tiles of a stop ride to jobs near the line's other stops. Metro stations (4 tiles) all link up. Railways with train stations are fastest, and track to the map edge carries commuters to the region.
  9. Educate: schools (and later a university) raise the share of skilled residents over a few years. Offices, big shops and high-tech industry need skilled workers; skilled residents also pay more tax. Press N for the education overlay.
  10. Terrain: Hills and Coast maps (New city) have slopes: roads cost more there and hillside buildings stay low, but high ground has views. Raise / Lower land reshape open land, fill water or dig it.
  11. Scenery: the ☾ and 🍂 buttons in the top bar turn the day/night cycle and the seasons on or off.
  12. Landmarks unlock as the city grows: a town park, a central park (1,000 residents), a university (1,500) and a stadium (2,500). They cover several tiles and lift a whole area; the stadium sells tickets.
  13. Districts: name neighbourhoods in the Districts panel, paint them, and give them rules: a height limit, no heavy industry, or a tax break.
  14. City hall (the card at the bottom left): follow goals for cash rewards, enact ordinances such as recycling or free transit, and watch your mayor rating: a high rating earns yearly grants and a statue. City → New city or scenario offers challenge maps.
  15. Mind the budget: roads and public buildings cost upkeep. Twelve months in debt and you're fired.

Staying solvent

  • Grow first, serve later. Start with a short street off the regional road, a strip of industry by the entrance, homes and a few shops. Taxes come from people and jobs.
  • Build roads only where you zone. Every road tile costs upkeep forever.
  • One wind farm + one pump by the river covers your first ~500 people. Add plants when the Power gauge turns orange, not before.
  • Add services as neighbourhoods fill: school, clinic, police, fire each cover a 9–10 tile radius. Around 500+ residents is a good time for the first ones.
  • Follow the demand panel. Zone whatever is positive, a little at a time.
  • In trouble? Open Last month: the advisor lists what's costing you. Borrow $10,000, bulldoze unused roads, or sell a service back (half refund).
